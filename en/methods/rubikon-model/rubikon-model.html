Crossing the Rubicon

Between wish and deed lies a river. Before the Rubicon you weigh things – open, deliberating. Afterward there is no going back: you plan, act, evaluate. Most projects fail because they never leave the bank. This tool takes you across.

1 · Weighing

Here you may still doubt. Collect reasons – and weigh them. One strong argument counts more than three weak ones.

Expectancy × value

Motivation comes from two questions: How important is it to me – and how likely am I to succeed?

2 · Crossing the Rubicon

From here the wish becomes an intention. Weighing is over – whoever keeps doubting after the decision never crosses the river.

Every decision is also a letting go. Name it – that prevents later regret.

3 · Planning

Gollwitzer's finding: Those who formulate Wenn-Dann-Pläne implement intentions two to three times more often. The if is a concrete situation, the then a concrete action.

4 · Acting

Now only doing counts. Log actions and check them off.

5 · Evaluating

Only now may you evaluate again. What is achieved, what is not – and what follows from that?

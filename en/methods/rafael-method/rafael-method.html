Learn from a situation – systematically

RAFAEL is a feedback and reflection method for concrete situations: a presentation, a difficult conversation, a meeting you led. Six steps take you from a sober report through alternatives and outside feedback to one to three learning steps you try next time.

R Report

The situation you want to reflect on – and first, what gut went well. This order is part of the method.

What kind of situation is this?
Like a camera: who was there, what was said, what did you do, how did people react.

What went well?

At least three points before you get to the weak spots.

What went less well?

Concrete moments – not “I was nervous,” but “on slide 4 I lost the thread and spoke too fast.”

6

A Alternatives

For each weak spot: what could you have done instead? Several options – even unrealistic ones. The choice comes later.

F Feedback

Now the others get a say. Get feedback from people who were there – or enter what you've already heard.

Feedback received

Self-image vs. how others see you

A Exchange

Sort every piece of feedback. Don't justify – sort. What matches your picture, what's new, where do you see it differently?

Follow-up questions

On points you see differently or don't understand: what do you still want to ask the person who gave feedback?

E Elaboration

Now we condense: which topics show up for you and in the feedback? What's the actual pattern behind it?

The pattern behind it

Often not “slide 4,” but “under pressure I lose contact with the audience.”

L Learning steps

One to three concrete things you'll do differently next time. Less is more – one step you actually take beats five good resolutions.

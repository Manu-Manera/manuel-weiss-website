The problem has a system

Systemic coaching does not look at you alone, but at the web of relationships, expectations and patterns in which a topic arises – and holds. You map your system, ask circular questions, recognize loops and plan a small experiment that interrupts the pattern.

Your concern

Context

Who belongs to the system?

Everyone connected to the topic – including people who are absent, whose expectations still play along. Closeness = how tightly you are connected, quality = how the relationship feels right now.

Circular questions

The heart of systemic work: You answer questions from other people's perspective. That makes differences and interactions visible that are invisible from your position.

The pattern loop

Problems in systems usually hold through a loop: I do A → the other does B → I do even more A … Rebuild your loop.

What is the problem good for?

An irritating but central question: Every stable pattern has a function in the system. It protects, stabilizes, prevents or enables something. For whom – and what?

Reframing

Give the problem behavior a new frame. Not sugarcoating – name the skill or intention behind it.

Resources in the system

Who or what in the system is a resource – even in an unexpected place?

Hypotheses

Systemic hypotheses are not truths, but useful guesses. Good is whatever opens new options for action. Write at least two – the more different, the better.

Pattern interruption

You cannot change others – but you can change your part of the loop. Choose where you start.
